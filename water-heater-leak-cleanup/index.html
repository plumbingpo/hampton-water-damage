Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Hampton

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Hampton crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWater from the tank can scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
  • The relief valve for temperature and pressure opening, or failing, and letting water out.
  • Rust at the connections above the tank, either the flex lines or the inlet and outlet fittings.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Attic and Ceilings

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.

Heater Closet Drywall

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Shared Garage Wall

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Items Stored Near the Heater

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • Where the heater sits, with attic and interior closet leaks costing more than garage leaks.
  • The volume of wet stored items that must be dried, moved or disposed of.
  • Flooring type in the neighboring rooms, where the water reached them.
  • Any rebuilding of the subfloor or platform beneath the tank ahead of the new heater.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
